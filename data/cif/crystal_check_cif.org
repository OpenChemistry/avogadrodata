#+TITLE: crystal_check_cif.org

To complement automated tests vs. the set of .cif on
https://github.com/openchemistry/crystals, a couple of structure models about
small organic compounds are compiled.  For reasons of copyright and terms on
permitted use, the .cif files' /only/ source is the Crystallographic Open Database
(COD), https://crystallography.net consulted [2026-10-13 Tue] on
https://crystallography.net/cod, http://qiserver.ugr.es/cod/, or
https://crystallography.io/.

There are 230 conventional settings of space groups.  CCDC reports how they
distribute in the CSD file (about [small molecule] organic/organometallic
structure models), a distribution different to the one in the ICSD (focused on
inorganic compounds).  Like the printed IT, the table below compiles the
conventional setting of a spacegroup (e.g., P2(1)/c) ahead of a
'nonconventional' / less conventional one (e.g., P2(1)/a, or P2(1)/n), yet still
by the same IT number.  Contrasting to the 230, there is no exhaustive
compilation of non-standard settings of spacegroups.

The focus of this first batch of structures are the 10 most frequently reported
spacegroups of structure models of the CSD file.  This covers about 90% of the
data eventually admitted.  The most recent survey by CCDC (see open access
compilation on
https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/media/CSD-Organic-Space-Group-Statistics-Space-Group-Frequency-Ordering-2026.pdf
stamped by [2026-01-01 Thu]) is used here as a guideline.

To ease review, where possible, .cif referenced were favored if

1) their primary publication is a journal by IUCr.  This presumes there is a
   higher propability data are checked (e.g., https://checkcif.iucr.org/ + human
   reviewers) for consistency and if necessary curated than in publications
   where results of chemical crystallography tend to be an auxiliary.
2) the model does not require to account a) for disorder and/or b) twinning
3) the publication is open access

Notes to the table:

1) The underlying structure is "the YLID", i.e. the probably most prominent
   reference to check single-crystal X-ray diffractometers for small molecules.
   The CSD file holds multiple records of this by structure code MSULIN
   (https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/structures/Search?Ccdcid=msulin&DatabaseToSearch=Published)
2) disordered methanol as solvent molecule present (O61)
3) Abstract explicitly states "However, in order to facilitate the analysis of
   the striking pseudosymmetry relating the four independent mol­ecules, the
   structure has been analysed and reported in the nonconventional centred
   B\overline{1} space-group setting."
4) Abstract explicitly states "The twin lattice coincides with the
   pseudo-hexagonal sublattice of the individual domain, which justifies the
   adoption of the unconventional setting B21 of the space group."
